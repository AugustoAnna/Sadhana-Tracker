Reminder conversion · small sample

Do reminders actually pull people back to log?

Share of a participant's logs that landed within three hours after one of their enabled reminder times — compared not against another group, but against what that same share would be if their logs were spread evenly through the day. Data limitation — read before trusting this number
Three things keep this directional rather than conclusive. First, converting a timestamp to "3 hours after a local reminder" needs the participant's timezone, which was added late (migration 004): only 33 of 223 participants have one, and they skew toward people who engaged with notifications. Second, reminder rows store current state with no timestamp, so a reminder set in week 3 is applied to week-1 logs as if it had always existed — this inflates the lift. Third, people plausibly set reminders at the times they already practise, so a 2.3× lift shows logs cluster around reminder times, not that reminders cause them. An earlier version of this section headlined "63% vs 35%" against a 4-person no-reminder group; that comparison wasn't like-for-like (a single best-case window vs several fixed ones) and has been withdrawn.

Reminders · setup & timing

Who turns reminders on, how many, and when

Reminders are collapsed by name and unioned across a person's device-rows, then deduped by (kind, slot, practice_id, time_local) — the same reminder syncing down to a second device shouldn't count twice. "Enabled" means the toggle is actually on (enabled=true), not just that a reminders row exists — visiting the screen alone can leave a disabled row behind. Practices allow up to 3 reminders in the UI (generic slots 1–3 plus per-practice reminders), though a person can exceed that by combining generic and per-practice reminders.

Gap trend · early vs late half

Are gaps between logs getting shorter or longer?

For each participant with at least 4 distinct logging days (≥3 gaps, enough to split into a real early half and late half), this compares the mean gap length in the first half of their chronological gap sequence to the second half. Shortening/lengthening requires the late-half mean to differ from the early-half mean by 20% or more, relative; anything closer counts as steady. For odd counts the middle gap is dropped, so at the 3-gap minimum this compares one gap with one — read the small-count cases as anecdote, not trend.

Most people (21 of 34, 62%) keep a roughly consistent gap length across the study, and the aggregate median barely moves — 1.00 day early-half vs 1.11 days late-half. But the mean nearly doubles (1.36 → 2.73 days), because a handful of low-gap-count participants had one late blowup dominate their late half: Minakshi (gaps of 1, 2, then 23 days), Deepthi Rajashekar Johal (1, 1, 2, then 13), and Srinidhi (1, 1, then 6) all show lengthening of 400–2200% — dramatic on paper, but each is really "one big late gap" rather than a steady drift. The clearest shortening case is Anu Sharma, whose gaps fell from an early mean of 6.0 days to a late mean of 3.5. Participants with the longest track records — Suneel, Neha Sharma, and Swami Gana among them, each with 30+ gaps that are nearly all length 1 — are steady almost by construction: with that much day-to-day consistency there's little room left to drift either way.
Terminal lapse · stopped for good

Who stopped logging and never came back?

A participant is in "terminal lapse" once 14 or more days have passed since their last log, as of Sep 15 — long enough that a genuine break becomes unlikely to still be mid-gap rather than gone for good. For each, "day of use they stopped on" is (last log date − first log date) + 1 — which day of their own usage lifecycle their final log landed on, regardless of how many days in between were actually active.
