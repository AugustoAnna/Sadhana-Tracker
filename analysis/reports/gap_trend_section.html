Are gaps between logs getting shorter or longer?

For each participant with at least 4 distinct logging days (≥3 gaps, enough to split into a real early half and late half), this compares the mean gap length in the first half of their chronological gap sequence to the second half. Shortening/lengthening requires the late-half mean to differ from the early-half mean by 20% or more, relative; anything closer counts as steady.

Reading this chart
Most people (21 of 34, 62%) keep a roughly consistent gap length across the study, and the aggregate median barely moves — 1.00 day early-half vs 1.11 days late-half. But the mean nearly doubles (1.36 → 2.73 days), because a handful of low-gap-count participants had one late blowup dominate their late half: Minakshi (gaps of 1, 2, then 23 days), Deepthi Rajashekar Johal (1, 1, 2, then 13), and Srinidhi (1, 1, then 6) all show lengthening of 400–2200% — dramatic on paper, but each is really "one big late gap" rather than a steady drift. The clearest shortening case is Anu Sharma, whose gaps fell from an early mean of 6.0 days to a late mean of 3.5. Participants with the longest track records — Suneel, Neha Sharma, and Swami Gana among them, each with 30+ gaps that are nearly all length 1 — are steady almost by construction: with that much day-to-day consistency there's little room left to drift either way.
