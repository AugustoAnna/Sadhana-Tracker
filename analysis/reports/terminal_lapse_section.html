Who stopped logging and never came back?

A participant is in "terminal lapse" once 14 or more days have passed since their last log, as of Sep 15 — long enough that a genuine break becomes unlikely to still be mid-gap rather than gone for good. For each, "day of use they stopped on" is (last log date − first log date) + 1 — which day of their own usage lifecycle their final log landed on, regardless of how many days in between were actually active.

Reading this chart
6 of the 13 terminal-lapse participants — nearly half — logged exactly once, on day 1, and never returned: Vijayaruban, Kirti, Practices of Neha Sharma, Mradul, Yuyu, and Shrabanti Mazumdar. That's by far the single most common terminal-lapse pattern — a one-time try, not a habit that formed and then broke. A smaller group stuck around much longer before dropping off: Shweta Sharma (day 16) and Saminder Paul (day 15) both logged across two-plus weeks before going silent 15–19 days ago. For contrast, the larger group — 31 of 44 named loggers (70%) — logged within the last 14 days and are still plausibly active, so terminal lapse so far describes a minority, concentrated heavily among one-and-done first tries rather than people who built a routine and then quit it.
